## 2. New material: the ICLR 2027 cohort (all ABSTRACT level)

Twenty HC-family / widened-residual submissions that appear in no project write-up. Ordered by
relevance to (a)-(c).

### 2.1 **`uHC: Uncoupled Hyper-Connections`** (`Qnj7Lf8Bz2`, 2026-09-19) — the closest published item to design A. ABSTRACT

Verbatim, the framing: *"existing HC architectures maintain coupled interfaces between residual
streams and the transformation blocks: **readout patterns are shared across feature dimensions**, and
write updates remain aligned across streams, limiting the benefits of residual-stream expansion. We
identify this transformation-interface coupling as a structural bottleneck."*

Their fix, verbatim: *"uHC introduces **Group-Wise Feature Readout** for feature-dependent stream
aggregation and **Low-Rank Directional Write-Back** for stream-specific update directions with
lightweight overhead."*
TL;DR: *"uHC uncouples the read and write interfaces of Hyper-Connections, enabling more effective use
of multiple residual streams and consistently improving language-modeling performance."*

Results stated: *"Across three model scales ranging from 46M to 363M parameters, uHC consistently
improves validation loss and perplexity over residual connections, mHC, and mHC-lite. At the largest
scale, uHC achieves the best performance across all reported metrics, reducing validation loss from
**2.9470** for mHC to **2.8188**."* Plus *"uHC better utilizes the expanded residual-stream space and
achieves **more stable optimization**"*.

**Why this is the central finding of this re-check.** Design A = per-channel-group `H_pre` +
per-channel-group `H_post` + per-channel-group doubly stochastic `H_res`. uHC claims the first of the
three, under the name this project would have used, with a group axis, **at 46M-363M, i.e. the same
dense small-scale regime as this project's exp09-exp15**. The reported mHC -> uHC delta of 0.128 nats
at 363M is an order of magnitude larger than any structural head effect this project measured at
d384/d768, which is itself a reason to read the full text before the report leans either way.

**What it does not claim.** The abstract never mentions `H_res`, Sinkhorn, the Birkhoff polytope,
double stochasticity or a per-group mixing matrix; the write is described as *stream*-specific
(low-rank directions), not group-specific. So on the evidence available:
design A's distinguishing component — a **per-group** `n x n` doubly stochastic residual mix — remains
unclaimed, but **"each channel group gets its own read" is no longer a novel proposal.**
Unresolved, and only resolvable from the full text: whether the groups are contiguous blocks, how many
there are, whether the group count is swept, whether the read coefficients are predicted per token,
and whether the comparison is parameter-matched.

### 2.2 **`Multi-Head Attention Residuals`** (`tHhLEKa9YL`, 2026-09-08) — MHAR's attribution has flipped. ABSTRACT, compared against arXiv v2 FULL-TEXT

Same paper as arXiv 2607.27230, but the OpenReview abstract is **newer** (2026-09-08 vs v2's
2026-07-31) and the causal claim is reversed.

| | arXiv v2 (2026-07-31) | ICLR 2027 (2026-09-08) |
|---|---|---|
| attribution | *"A direct probe of the trained queries **confirms that learned subspace disagreement is the underlying driver**."* | *"**Temperature-matched controls attribute most of the gain to the flatter per-head softmax the reshape provides automatically** — a single query's depth softmax over-sharpens as width grows — **with a small residual consistent with per-subspace routing**."* |
| losses | −0.061 / −0.149 / −0.140 at 100M/350M/1B | identical |
| U-shape | flat optimum H=4-8, adopt H=8 | identical |
| 8B mid-training | +3.2 GSM8K, +3.1 GPQA | identical |

Everything else in the two abstracts matches. So the numbers stand; the *mechanism* does not.
On MHAR's own newest telling, **most of what looks like a per-subspace-routing gain is a softmax
temperature artifact** — splitting one query into `H` heads flattens each head's depth distribution,
and that flattening, not the subspace independence, carries most of the effect. Note that the
temperature story has no analogue in design A: mHC's `H_pre`/`H_post` are sigmoid-gated and `H_res` is
Sinkhorn-normalised, with no softmax over a growing width to over-sharpen. This cuts **against**
transferring MHAR's success to design A, and it independently reduces the tension the 10-03 note
flagged between MHAR's tuned-LR result and this project's exp10.

### 2.3 Evidence bearing on (b) — LR tolerance and spike damping

#### 2.3.1 **`One Marginal Is Enough: What Doubly Stochastic Routing Buys Hyper-Connections, and What It Costs`** (`1BC0eYN2uS`, 2026-09-17). ABSTRACT

The sharpest published statement yet on what `H_res`'s constraint is worth. Verbatim:
*"**Fixing either one alone already bounds the spectral norm of the whole product by `sqrt(n)`, at any
depth**, so a single marginal buys the depth-uniform stability the constraint was introduced for. The
second only tightens that bound from `sqrt(n)` to 1, and it is not free: the mixer can then neither
replicate a source nor aggregate at a destination, and **it can only preserve or dissipate the
differences among the streams, never enlarge them**."* Their **osHC** keeps one marginal and releases
the other, in four variants (two fixing the same marginal at every layer, two switching partway), all
proved stable at every depth. Results: *"Across ten routing constructions, two pretraining corpora and
three scales, osHC attains the best validation loss in five of the six settings and **beats every
exactly doubly stochastic construction in all six**"*, best and second-best zero-shot perplexity on
five held-out corpora at the largest scale, and *"Trained osHC routers use the released capability at
almost every site"*.

Relevance: design A's novelty is a *per-group* exactly doubly stochastic mix. osHC argues the second
marginal is the expensive half of exactly that constraint, and that the stability argument survives
without it. The "can only preserve or dissipate stream differences" claim is the same mechanism oHC
(2609.02672) proves and DepthBench (2609.32534) measures as effective-rank collapse — now a
three-paper convergence.

#### 2.3.2 **`On the Design of Hyper-Connections: Simplification, Stability, and Scalability`** (SimpleHC, `i2WyUVJUJ2`, 2026-09-06). ABSTRACT

Verbatim: *"We find that **learned read/write gating without mixing** and **unconstrained mixing with
fixed gates** form two effective interaction groups: **either alone achieves performance comparable to
mHC**. We also identify repeated access to the expanded residual state as a major source of training
overhead, even without mixing."* Their **SimpleHC** *"simplifies the architecture by **removing
residual mixing**"* and shares memory access across layer-group gates. *"SimpleHC incurs only **2.4%**
extra end-to-end training time over a vanilla residual baseline at the largest tested scale. It
maintains performance comparable to mHC when scaling from **15B to 30B MoE** models, exhibits
**smoother gradient dynamics** during standard training, and **remains stable under stress**."*

Relevance to (b) and (c): a fourth independent design that deletes `H_res`, and a direct
read/write-gating-vs-mixing decomposition on the mHC operator. "Remains stable under stress" is
exactly the LR-tolerance axis, though the abstract does not say what the stress test is.

#### 2.3.3 Two architecture-independent loss-spike papers — a caution, not a counter-result

- **`Spiking, Fast and Slow: The Anatomy of Loss Spikes in Language Model Pretraining`**
  (`ptNmbmCdj3`, 2026-09-13). ABSTRACT. Splits instabilities into *"fast spikes"* and *"slow terminal
  divergences ... which we call **time-bomb spikes**"*, and attributes both to LM-head logit growth
  interacting with Adam's preconditioner under finite precision: *"various mitigation techniques from
  the literature (including our own) largely act on the shared underlying problem of logit growth by
  reducing quantization error in the LM head."*
- **`Understanding and Mitigating AdamW Loss Spikes`** (`pAjrfibDB9`, 2026-09-18). ABSTRACT.
  Attributes spikes to first/second-moment time-scale mismatch; at spike onset the median fraction of
  coordinates with `|m/(sqrt(v)+eps)| > 1` is 0.571% versus 0.0033% at ordinary states (>170x); their
  dual control takes post-warmup spike episodes *"from 17 to zero"* at GPT-2 774M and improves mean
  validation loss 2.916 -> 2.856.

Relevance: neither mentions HC or mHC. They matter because the report's (b) claim — that the heads
damp gradient spikes — now has two well-measured architecture-independent explanations competing with
it (LM-head logit growth; optimizer moment mismatch), on top of Qwen's multiplicative-gate-as-
rescaling explanation from 10-03 §3.1. A spike-damping result at d384 should rule these out before
being attributed to connection granularity.

#### 2.3.4 **`Do Hyper-Connections Transfer to Reinforcement Learning?`** (SHC-PPO, `dzXT0zmzQn`, 2026-09-17). ABSTRACT

Only tangential to (b), but a clean instance of the distinction this project draws between "stable"
and "better". Verbatim: *"hyper-connections degrade PPO on the highest-dimensional continuous control
tasks, precisely those where the gradient-routing argument predicts the largest gain, while the mixing
norm drifts in every run"*; and at six layers *"they separate on **optimization geometry rather than on
return**: SHC-PPO preserves gradient-norm anisotropy two orders of magnitude better than unconstrained
mixing ... **while returns across the four conditions remain within each other's confidence
intervals**."* Their closing line is quotable for the report: *"Where a constraint is enforced is
therefore measurable in the optimization geometry at depths where it is invisible in the reward."*

### 2.4 Evidence bearing on (c) — parameter-matched / iso-compute HC comparisons

#### 2.4.1 **`Multi-Stream Attention Residuals`** (MSAR, `Eqb6662X3z`, 2026-08-20). ABSTRACT

Multi-head depth attention at frontier scale, and a new head-to-head HC-variant comparison.
Verbatim: *"Inspired by the success of multi-head attention along the sequence axis, we introduce
Multi-Stream Attention Residuals (MSAR), an extension of AttnRes that generalizes this mechanism to
**multi-head depth attention** ... MSAR draws stream diversity from sequence context through
**multi-scale causal convolutions**, allowing different heads to retrieve historical representations
through distinct contextual views."* Results: *"Across all 12 downstream benchmarks at the **18B and
28B MoE** scales, MSAR consistently outperforms prior residual variants, including standard residual
connections, manifold-constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC), and AttnRes. Scaling-law experiments yield
an **iso-loss compute speedup of 1.58x** over the standard residual baseline, compared with **1.35x**
for AttnRes and **1.30x** for mHC."*

Relevance to (a) and (c): the heads are over *contextual views* produced by convolutions, **not over
channel groups** — so it is not prior art for design A — but it is (i) a second independent
"multi-head depth routing beats single-head" result at 10-30x the scale of MHAR's, and (ii) the
cleanest iso-loss three-way HC-variant ranking in the literature. The report's (c) section should
carry the 1.58 / 1.35 / 1.30 triple.

#### 2.4.2 **`Delta Attention Residuals`** (`4ERlcNAeHJ`, 2026-09-08). ABSTRACT

A strong negative scaling result for the AttnRes family that the notes do not carry. Verbatim:
*"We find that this replacement limits AttnRes at scale: it improves on standard residuals at 220M but
is **6.9% worse in perplexity at 1044M and 6.6% worse at 7.6B**, and the variant without any residual
stream degrades most."* Their fix keeps the residual stream and routes **deltas** `v_i = h_{i+1} - h_i`;
cumulative hidden states are *"highly redundant (adjacent cosine 0.93-0.97, R^2 up to 0.99 in deep
layers)"*. Delta AttnRes improves 1.7-8.2% over standard residuals across 220M-7.6B.

Relevance: MHAR's parent method **regresses** at >=1B in someone else's hands. Combined with MHAR's own
Appendix Table 10 (single-head AttnRes +0.055 at 350M and +0.140 at 1B on FineWeb-Edu, 10-03 §4.1),
the report should treat the single-head AttnRes baseline as unreliable above ~350M. The adjacent-cosine
0.93-0.97 figure also parallels this project's own copy-similarity probe (cosine 0.76-0.87).

### 2.5 **`Inside the Residual Stream: Neuron-Level Mixing with Nonlinear Low-Rank Branches`** (ResLorB, `S6Dmafpu1o`, 2026-08-23) — design A's gap, stated in public. ABSTRACT

Verbatim: *"Methods such as Hyper-Connection and Attention Residuals enrich connectivity by
introducing interactions among different hidden states. However, **these approaches primarily operate
at the level of whole hidden states, leaving finer-grained interactions within individual states
largely unexplored**. Frac-Connection explores finer-grained connectivity by partitioning hidden
states into multiple streams and applying structured interactions, but **its interaction pattern is
constrained by a fixed coordinate-wise decomposition**."*

Their answer is **not** design A: `ResLorB` adds *"a lightweight nonlinear low-rank transformation at
the block input to enable learnable neuron-level interactions"*, compatible with mHC, and the low-rank
branch *"can further serve as an additional interactable state"*. Results on DeepSeek-V3-style models
up to 3B/9B: 0.011 reduction in training LM loss and 1.4% downstream average at 9B, *"only 1%
additional parameters and negligible latency overhead"*.

Relevance: the report's motivation paragraph can now **cite** the gap rather than assert it — and must
also note that one group has already answered the same diagnosis with a different mechanism, and that
"combining ResLorB with existing multi-state architectures further yields non-saturating gains".

## 3. Code re-checks

### 3.1 **Correction to `recheck_2026-10-03.md` §4.3**: `lucidrains/hyper-connections` has two mHC classes with different `num_fracs` algebra. CODE

The 10-03 note reports a single reading: *"The mix is therefore one `(n m) x (n m)` doubly stochastic
matrix over the joint index — design D — with no option to restrict it to block-diagonal (design A)."*
Re-read today, the repository (unchanged since 2026-05-13) contains **two** separate implementations:

- **`hyper_connections/mHCv2.py`** — the 10-03 reading is **correct here**. `sinkhorn_knopps` opens
  with `assert log_alpha.shape[-2] == log_alpha.shape[-1]`; the read/residual split is
  `alpha[..., :num_input_views * num_fracs]` / `alpha[..., num_input_views * num_fracs:]`; and the mix
  is `einsum('... s f tf, ... s f d -> ... tf d')`, i.e. one square map over the **joint** `(stream,
  frac)` index of size `n*m`. Design D, no block-diagonal option. This is the class the published
  variants build on, so the report's `m > 1` initialisation sentence stands.
- **`hyper_connections/manifold_constrained_hyper_connections.py`** — different algebra. Its
  `sinkhorn_knopps` has **no square assert** and alternates `l1norm(dim=-2)` / `l1norm(dim=-1)`;
  `static_alpha = cat((init_alpha0, eye(n*m)), dim=-1)` is rearranged `'(f s) d -> f s d'`; the
  constraint is applied to a **rectangular** slice spanning both the stream and the fraction axis; and
  the mix is `einsum('... f1 s f2 t, ... f1 s d -> ... f2 t d')`, i.e. **every input fraction feeds
  every output fraction**. Note also that with `num_fracs > 1` this class splits the read off as
  `alpha[..., :self.num_input_views]` where `mHCv2.py` uses
  `self.num_input_views * self.num_fracs` — the slice does not factor cleanly for `m > 1`, so this
  path looks buggy for fractions and should not be used as evidence of anyone's intended design.

Either way the conclusion is unchanged and now rests on two code paths instead of one: **neither class
offers a block-diagonal (per-group) `H_res`.** Design A is not implementable in this repository without
new code.

### 3.2 `anonymous.4open.science/r/cmhc` — the one ICLR 2027 submission whose code is readable. CODE

cmHC's anonymous repository (README, `model_cmhc.py`, `train_cmhc.py`, `hyper_conn/mhc.py`,
`hyper_conn/hyper_connections.py`, `ascend-cmhc-core/`) is a nanoGPT fork vendoring lucidrains'
`ManifoldConstrainedHyperConnections`, with the contraction hyperparameters exposed as
`--cmhc_gamma` (their `lambda`, default 1) and `--cmhc_gamma_num_layers`. Baselines are run from
`config/with_hc.py`, `with_mhc.py`, `with_mhc_lite.py`, `with_cmhc_lite.py`; streams via
`--hyper_conn_n` (default 4); OpenWebText and FineWeb-Edu.

Two things worth recording:

1. **They changed the predictor's shape, and the comment says so**:
   `# XXX we modified the original implementation here / H_res is from nC to n^2, instead of from
   (n, C) to (n, n)`. Their `dynamic_alpha_fn` output width is
   `num_fracs * (n*n + n*num_input_views)` and their forward does
   `rearrange(alpha_residual, '... f s g t -> ... f g s t')` before a log-domain Sinkhorn over the
   last two axes — so with `m > 1` this code builds **`m^2` independent `n x n` doubly stochastic
   blocks**, one per `(frac_in, frac_out)` pair, with `mix_h` reading every input fraction into every
   output fraction.
2. **That is still not design A.** Design A is the *diagonal* subset of those blocks (`m` blocks, one
   per group, no cross-group mixing). cmHC's structure is strictly richer, and the resulting
   `(nm) x (nm)` matrix has row and column sums `m`, not 1 — it is not globally doubly stochastic.
   The Ascend kernel README confirms the intended per-token controls are stream-level only:
   *"Produces the branch input `hIn`, gating **vector** `hPost`, and the doubly-stochastic residual
   **matrix** `hRes`"* — no channel axis.

So the nearest thing to a per-group doubly stochastic mix that exists in any code I can read is a
dense `m x m` block structure, reached by a shape change its own authors flag as a deviation.

### 3.3 One new-to-the-project code item: a third-party mHC Triton kernel. CODE

`woct0rdho/transformers5-qwen3.5-recipe`, file `deepseek_v4_liger_mhc.py` (created 2026-07-21, last
pushed 2026-09-13 — **not new, newly noticed**; surfaced by the `mhc_heads` code query). Fused Triton
forward/backward mHC kernels for low-VRAM LoRA training against
`transformers.models.deepseek_v4.modeling_deepseek_v4`. Relevant details:

- `_HC = 4`, `_HIDDEN = 4096`, `_SINKHORN_ITERS = 20`, and crucially
  **`_MIX = (_HC + 2) * _HC`** — the per-token control block is `n^2 + 2n = 24` values, i.e. `H_res`
  (`n x n`) plus `H_pre` (`n`) plus `H_post` (`n`), **all per-stream scalars with no channel axis**.
- Another **naming-collision** instance for `web_and_code_search.md` §5: `EXPECTED_DEEPSEEK_V4_MHC_HEADS
  = 1` and the repeated phrase "DeepSeek V4 mHC head" refer to `DeepseekV4HyperHead`, the fused
  read-out module — not to any channel grouping. This is the third independent place (after DeepSeek's
  own TileKernels and `waefrebeorn/wubuwizard`) where "mHC head" means something other than design A.

Verdict: strong corroboration that implementers working against production mHC see **no** head axis on
the channel dimension.

## 4. Corrections and refinements to earlier claims

1. **`recheck_2026-10-03.md` §6 said OpenReview was "not re-censused this round" and that ICLR 2027
   submissions "would not necessarily be public yet."** They are public and searchable through
   `api2.openreview.net/notes/search`, and they are where the HC field has moved: ~20 HC-family
   submissions, **none on arXiv**. Any future re-check must query OpenReview first, not arXiv.
   The 10-03 note's conclusion that "the 09-26 crawl's real weakness was screening, not coverage"
   needs a second clause: **coverage was also bounded by searching arXiv and Semantic Scholar only.**
2. **"MHAR's results are the best prior for design A"** — needs the strongest qualification yet.
   MHAR's own newest abstract attributes *most* of the head-split gain to softmax temperature flattening
   rather than per-subspace routing (§2.2). Since mHC's read/write are sigmoid-gated and its mix is
   Sinkhorn-normalised, there is no softmax to over-sharpen, so the mechanism MHAR now credits does not
   transfer to design A at all. The report should not use MHAR as evidence that per-subspace routing works.
3. **The novelty claim for design A must be narrowed.** "Per-channel-group read coefficients for a
   widened residual stream" is proposed and tested in `uHC` (Group-Wise Feature Readout, 46M-363M,
   mHC 2.9470 -> 2.8188). What remains unclaimed is the **per-group doubly stochastic `H_res`** —
   and that is the component osHC argues is over-constrained, SimpleHC removes, and Qwen GR, Chimera
   iHC and SiHC also drop.
4. **`recheck_2026-10-03.md` §4.3's "one `(nm) x (nm)` doubly stochastic matrix" is right for
   `mHCv2.py` only**; the sibling class in the same repository has cross-fraction `(f1, f2)` algebra and
   an apparently buggy read slice for `m > 1` (§3.1).
5. **`web_and_code_search.md` §5 (naming collision) needs two additions**: `TaskHC` uses "functional
   **groups**" of hyper-connection *streams* for detection transformers, and
   `woct0rdho/transformers5-qwen3.5-recipe` is a third "mHC head" usage meaning the fused read-out
   module (§3.3). "Grouped hyper-connections" is now taken, in the stream sense.
6. **The "`H_res` adds little" tally is now five independent designs**, not three: Qwen GR
   (2608.30320), Chimera iHC (2607.28611), SiHC's identity carry (2609.33895), **SimpleHC**
   (`i2WyUVJUJ2`) and **osHC**'s released marginal (`1BC0eYN2uS`). Plus cmHC, Structured mHC, GDHC,
   ITO-HC and eHC all attack `H_res` from a different direction (contraction, objective choice,
   retention, signed off-diagonals, equilibrium writes) — the mix is the field's most contested
   component, which is both an opportunity and a crowding risk for design A.
7. **The AttnRes baseline is unreliable above ~350M.** `Delta Attention Residuals` reports it 6.9%
   worse than standard residuals at 1044M and 6.6% worse at 7.6B, consistent with MHAR's own Appendix
   Table 10. Any (c) comparison that uses single-head AttnRes as the reference should say so.
8. **New frontier-scale calibration for mHC's standalone gain**: RadFree reports mHC alone at **+1.29
   points** average downstream over a standard residual baseline at up to 125B MoE. Useful next to
   Qwen's 50.91 -> 54.47 and DepthBench's 400M/1.6B numbers.
9. **For (b), the report should rule out two architecture-independent spike mechanisms** before
   attributing damping to the heads: LM-head logit growth under finite precision (`ptNmbmCdj3`) and
   AdamW first/second-moment time-scale mismatch (`pAjrfibDB9`). §2.3.3.

## 6. Bottom line

**Design A still unpublished as of 2026-10-04: yes** — no source gives each channel group its own
read, write **and** its own `n x n` doubly stochastic residual mix.

**But the margin has narrowed sharply, and not from arXiv.** The ICLR 2027 cohort went public on
OpenReview and contains ~20 HC-family papers that none of this project's write-ups carry, none of them
on arXiv. Among them, **`uHC: Uncoupled Hyper-Connections`** names design A's premise verbatim
(*"readout patterns are shared across feature dimensions"*), fixes it with **Group-Wise Feature
Readout**, and reports mHC 2.9470 -> 2.8188 at 363M — this project's own scale. Design A's per-group
*read* is therefore no longer a novel proposal; only the per-group `H_res` is.

**And the per-group `H_res` is the component the field is abandoning.** `One Marginal Is Enough` proves
one marginal already buys the `sqrt(n)` depth-uniform stability bound and shows one-sided variants beat
every exactly doubly stochastic construction in all six settings; `SimpleHC` reports that gating
without mixing matches mHC at 15B-30B MoE with 2.4% overhead; that makes five independent designs that
drop `H_res` outright, plus five more that reparameterise it.

**On (b):** nothing supports a general "mHC/heads shift the usable LR upward" claim, and there are now
three competing architecture-independent explanations for spike damping (gate-as-rescaling, LM-head
logit growth, AdamW moment mismatch). **On (c):** two new numbers worth carrying — MSAR's iso-loss
speedups 1.58x / 1.35x / 1.30x (MSAR / AttnRes / mHC) at 18B-28B MoE, and RadFree's +1.29 points for
mHC alone at up to 125B.

**Most consequential single correction:** MHAR's own attribution flipped between arXiv v2 and its ICLR
2027 submission — *"Temperature-matched controls attribute most of the gain to the flatter per-head
softmax"*, not to per-subspace routing. The report's strongest external support for splitting the read
into subspaces has been withdrawn by its own authors, and the mechanism they now credit has no analogue
in mHC's sigmoid-gated, Sinkhorn-normalised operator.
