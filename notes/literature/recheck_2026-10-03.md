# Literature re-check, 2026-10-03

Re-check of the prior art for **design A** (mHC with the hidden dimension cut into `h` contiguous
groups, each group getting its own `H_pre`, `H_post` and its own `n x n` doubly stochastic `H_res`,
predicted per token) and for the four questions the report now rests on:

- **(a)** channel-grouped / multi-head / per-subspace connection coefficients in HC/mHC-like architectures;
- **(b)** evidence on whether finer-grained (per-channel, per-group) residual mixing helps;
- **(c)** HC/mHC training stability vs learning rate (the project's exp10 result: the heads mostly buy
  tolerance of a too-high LR and damp gradient spikes rather than lower loss at a tuned LR);
- **(d)** parameter-matched comparisons for HC variants (the project's finding that the global-predictor
  heads' gain is fully explained by their extra parameters).

Baselines not repeated here: `citation_crawl.md` (2026-09-26, 19 seeds, 186 unique citing papers) and
`web_and_code_search.md` including its 09-27 (§5) and 09-29 (§7) re-checks.
Everything below was read at the source unless the line says otherwise; levels are
**FULL-TEXT** (abstract + relevant method/experiment sections read), **ABSTRACT**, or **METADATA**.

---

## 0. Bottom line

- **Design A is still unpublished as of 2026-10-03: yes.** No paper, preprint, repository, blog or
  forum post proposes or tests per-channel-group connection coefficients for HC/mHC with a
  per-group doubly stochastic residual mix.
- **Nothing new appeared on arXiv between 2026-09-28 and 2026-10-03.** The newest genuine HC-family
  paper is still 2609.33895 (2026-09-27, found in the 09-29 re-check). Date-windowed queries returned
  only interpretability and unrelated work.
- **The one genuinely new item in the window is code, not a paper:** `wdlctc/hyper-connection-factory`
  pushed 303M and 1.21B parameter-controlled results on 2026-09-29 and 2026-10-02, including an
  independent MHAR-vs-single-head-AttnRes comparison (§2.2).
- **Closest new item overall** (new *to this project*, not new to arXiv): **Qwen3.8-Next
  (arXiv 2608.30320, 2026-08-31)**. Its *Gated Residual* is design A's **per-channel limit of the read
  only**, on a 4-branch widened residual stream, with a per-branch scalar write and **`H_res` dropped
  entirely**; and it reports exactly the three ablations this project needs: read granularity helps,
  write granularity does not, and `H_res` "adds little". It also runs an explicit over-LR stress test in
  which the gated configuration records zero loss spikes. This was missed before because its abstract
  never says "hyper-connections" or "mHC" (§3.1).
- Two further missed items bear directly on (b) and (c): **2601.22966** (Qwen, GatedNorm) has a clean
  elementwise-vs-tensorwise gating ablation in which the finer granularity wins; **2606.05014**
  (Depth-Attention) does per-head softmax depth mixing but shows that **most** of its gain comes from
  mixing at all, not from the per-head granularity (2.2348 -> 2.2153 uniform -> 2.2115 per-head).
- Methodological precedents the report should cite for (c)/(d): **2606.31859** (Review Residuals:
  parameter-matched, multi-seed, per-channel input-dependent gate — null to negative below 320M,
  significant only at 590M-1B) and **2609.32534** (DepthBench: per-architecture LR sweeps; mHC's
  optimal LR equals Pre-LN's).
- **Three independent large-scale designs now drop `H_res` altogether** — Qwen3.8-Next's GR
  (2608.30320), Chimera's iHC (2607.28611) and SiHC's identity carry (2609.33895) — which converges
  with this project's own observation that the residual mix contributes little.
- **Corrections** to earlier claims are in §5. The most important one: in MHAR's own setting the head
  split's gain is **not** only LR tolerance — it survives per-method LR tuning, 3x training budget and
  three paired seeds (§4.1). That does not contradict this project's exp10 (different mechanism, scale
  and base architecture), but the report should not generalise "heads only buy LR tolerance" beyond
  mHC-with-copies at d384.

## 3. Items the earlier searches missed (the substance of this re-check)

### 3.1 **arXiv 2608.30320** — "On the Design of Qwen3.8-Next Architecture: Evaluation, Efficiency, and Training Stability" (Qwen Team, 36 authors, cs.CL, 2026-08-31). FULL-TEXT (§2.2, §3.2-3.3)

Why it was missed, precisely: it **was** in the 2026-09-26 citation crawl's raw results — it appears in
`notes/literature/raw/s2_cites_2512.24880_off0.json` and `..._2409.19606_off0.json` — but it was dropped
during title/abstract triage and appears in neither write-up. Its abstract says the "residual stream is
widened to four branches and read through an elementwise gate" and never says hyper-connections, mHC,
head, group or channel, so no phrase query could recover it either. The same is true of three other
items below: **2606.05014** (in `s2_cites_2502.12170_off0.json`), **2606.31859** (in
`s2_cites_2603.15031_off0.json`) and **2607.28611** (in both mHC and HC citation files). The gap was in
screening, not in coverage.

What it does. It restates HC/mHC in its own notation — `x = H_mix^T R`,
`R' = H_res R + H_combine y^T`, each operator `= static + lambda * phi(R_bar W)`, and states
"HC uses phi = tanh with lambda init 0.01; mHC uses a sigmoid and additionally constrains `H_res` to a
manifold of doubly stochastic matrices" — then ablates the design space on 25B-A3B MoE models trained
on 560B tokens with `n_r = 4`. Verbatim conclusions:

- "**Bounded positive gates.** A sigmoid gate is better than tanh in both loss and training stability.
  This agrees with mHC."
- "**Data dependence.** Making `H_mix` and `H_combine` data-dependent reduces loss by 0.002 over the
  static variant, whereas the static variant reduced loss by 0.021 over the pre-norm baseline. The
  benchmark gain reverses this ratio: 1.98 points from static to dynamic against 1.58 from baseline to
  static. This is one of several places in this report where loss and downstream accuracy do not move
  together."
- "**Read granularity matters more than write granularity.** Refining `H_mix` ... from one scalar per
  branch to one weight per branch and channel helps. The same refinement of `H_combine` ... gives
  almost nothing, so the write stays a per-branch scalar."
- "**Read all branches.** Predicting the operators from all branches is better than using only the last
  branch or pooling the branches first. Normalizing each branch separately, that is, a group RMSNorm
  over the widened stream, gives a further gain."
- "**`H_res` adds little.** Once the read and the write are expressive enough, adding the
  `n_r x n_r` mixing operator brings no significant improvement."

Their resulting design, **Gated Residual (GR)**: per-branch RMSNorm with its own gain
`gamma_i in R^d`; a bottleneck predictor (rank `r = d/8`)
`G = unvec sigma(W_u SiLU((1/n_r) W_d vec(R_hat))) in R^{n_r x d}`; read
`x = (1/n_r) sum_i G_i * R_hat_i`; write `s = 2 sigma((1/n_r) W_w vec(R_hat)) in R^{n_r}`,
`R'_i = R_i + s_i y`. No `H_res`, no Sinkhorn. They place GR explicitly in this family: "GR belongs to
the same family as HC, mHC and VWN ... GR spends its expressiveness on the read: the gate is
elementwise, and `H_res` is dropped altogether, which the ablation shows costs nothing."

Table 5 (25B-A3B MoE, 560B tokens): pre-norm loss 1.617 / benchmark avg 50.91; mHC static 1.596 /
52.49; mHC dynamic 1.594 / 54.47; **GR 1.590 / 54.66**.
Table 6 (28 layers): pre-norm 1.789 (1.787 + GatedNorm); Block AttnRes S=4 1.773/1.768; S=2
1.770/1.766; Full AttnRes 1.762/**1.758**; GR (`n_r=4`) 1.762, with GatedNorm. At 48 layers Block
AttnRes S=4 1.711 vs GR 1.707.

Stability / learning rate, i.e. question (c), measured directly: they hold the LR at multiples of the
optimum on a 28-layer MoE and count loss spikes (steps exceeding a 201-step rolling median by > 0.1)
and clipping-threshold crossings. At 2x optimal LR the AdamW baseline spikes 4.3 per 10k steps vs 0.2
for Muon; at 4x the AdamW run spikes 183 per 10k and crosses the clipping threshold on 213 of 19,932
steps, while both Muon runs never cross and **the gated-residual configuration records zero loss
spikes**. A GatedNorm on/off toggle at 3x optimal LR takes the spike rate from 32.0 to 3.2 per 10k and
crossings from 256 to 20. Their explanation: "activation outliers grow almost proportionally with the
learning rate, while the spike rate grows much faster"; "training at high learning rates requires a
rescaling mechanism: without an explicit gate, the network achieves this by growing activation
outliers, leaving it fragile; the multiplicative gate supplies the necessary rescaling directly";
"GR supplies a rescaling that markedly improves training stability, and that stability margin shifts
the optimal learning rate and batch size upward"; and dropping `H_res`, "which requires separate
constraints, removes a potential source of instability".

They also decompose branch usage: with no `H_res` each branch is a plain accumulator, so
`a_{u->v} = (1/n_r) sum_c G_c^{(v)} * gamma_c * s_c^{(u)} y^{(u)} / rms(R_c^{(v)})` is exact (shares
sum to 1 within 3e-8). Across five GR checkpoints exactly one branch carries the long-range paths
(typical skip 10.9 layers vs 3.4-3.9 for the others); the layer-0 GDN -> layer-15 attention share rises
from 0.020 to 0.138 over training.

Relation to design A, stated carefully. GR is **design A's read half taken to `h = D`**, on a widened
(not copied) 4-branch stream, *static in the write* and with **no `H_res` at all**. It is the strongest
published evidence for (b) on the read path — and simultaneously the strongest published evidence
*against* investing in the thing design A makes per-group (the doubly stochastic mix), and against
refining the write. It is also an independent instance of the project's mechanism for (c): a
multiplicative gate buys stability margin at high LR, and that margin moves the optimal LR up. It is
**not** design A: no per-group `H_res`, no block-diagonal structure, one gate over all channels rather
than `h` groups sharing coefficients, and a 25B-A3B MoE rather than a dense 100M-scale model.

### 3.2 **arXiv 2601.22966** — "A Unified View of Attention and Residual Sinks: Outlier-Driven Rescaling is Essential for Transformer Training" (Qiu et al., Qwen Team, 19 authors, cs.CL, 2026-01-30). FULL-TEXT

The source of GatedNorm, and the cleanest published granularity ablation for (b): they compare
"the gating granularity (elementwise (score shape d) versus tensorwise (score shape 1))" and report
that "elementwise gating with a sigmoid activation yields the most significant performance improvement
over the baseline. Tensorwise gating with sigmoid reduces residual sinks to a similar extent ... but
its final performance is close to the baseline and consistently inferior to the elementwise variant.
This suggests that while both granularities can mitigate outliers ... finer-grained (elementwise)
rescaling enables more effective modulation and thus better performance." Non-sigmoid activations
destabilise, and under *tensorwise* gating all non-sigmoid activations diverge.
GatedNorm itself is `y_g = sigma(W_up(swish(W_down(y))))`, `y' = y_g * y`, bottleneck `r = 16`,
+3.7M parameters (~2% of a 2B model) and ~5% latency, with parameter parity restored by trimming the
FFN — i.e. a **parameter-matched** granularity comparison, which is what (d) asks for.
They also report that DyT + gated attention "enables the model to train stably at the baseline's
learning rate (4.3e-3), though its optimal learning is 2e-3" — the same "tolerance vs optimum"
distinction this project draws.

Relation to A: same granularity axis (scalar vs per-channel, input-dependent, sigmoid-bounded), applied
to the sublayer output rather than to connection coefficients. No streams, no `H_res`.

### 3.3 **arXiv 2606.05014** — "Depth-Attention: Cross-Layer Value Mixing for Language Models" (Zeng et al., LUMIA Lab SJTU, 10 authors, cs.CL, v1 2026-06-03, v2 2026-06-13; code `LUMIA-Group/Depth-Attention`). FULL-TEXT

Mixes earlier layers' **values** inside attention with **per-head softmax weights** over a depth source
list (queries are averaged within each GQA group, so depth attention runs at KV-head resolution), with
**zero added parameters** and no new inference state beyond the KV cache.

The number that matters for (b) is their Table 5 (500M, same source list in all three rows):
vanilla 2.2348 -> **uniform mixing 2.2153** -> **per-head softmax 2.2115**. So mixing at all is worth
−0.0195 and the per-head granularity adds only **−0.0038** on top. They state it as "per-head softmax
depth mixing is more effective than a fixed average over the same source list", which is true but
small. Value-only mixing beats key-only (2.2354, i.e. no gain) and key-value (2.2198).
Head-to-head at 1.5B (Qwen3-style, zero-shot avg): vanilla PPL 8.17 / 51.26; **mHC 7.88 / 52.88**;
Attention Residuals 7.78 / 52.80; DenseFormer 7.88 / 52.66; Depth-Attention 7.77 / **53.60**. At 3B:
vanilla 7.55 / 53.76; mHC 7.30 / 54.68; AttnRes 7.27. Overhead +9.1% / +11.2% wall-clock, against
+321% / +308% for their reference mHC implementation.

Relation to A: an independent instance of **per-head routing coefficients** (a), on depth history
rather than on parallel streams, row-stochastic rather than doubly stochastic; and a *quantified*
decomposition of how much the head granularity itself buys, which is the honest comparison for A.

### 3.4 **arXiv 2606.31859** — "Review Residuals: Update-Conditioned Residual Gating for Transformers" (Kyle Kramer, NeraTech, cs.LG, 2026-06-30). FULL-TEXT

`h_l = h_{l-1} + r_l * u_l` with `r_l = sigma(W_r [RMSNorm(h_{l-1}), RMSNorm(u_l)]) in (0,1)^d`, i.e. a
per-channel input-dependent write gate. The reason to cite it is the methodology: **baselines are
parameter-matched by widening**, with 2-3 seeds per size, on TinyStories at 5 sizes. Mean validation
loss (Table 2): 60M Review 1.6891 / Highway 1.6923 / standard **1.6805** (the plain residual wins);
150M 1.5576 / 1.5625 / 1.5548; 320M 1.5001 / 1.5042 / 1.5037; **590M 1.4795 / 1.4889 / 1.4901**
(significant, t = 3.3 vs Highway and 3.7 vs standard); 1B 1.4876 / 1.5031 / 1.5040 (+0.016 nats, trend,
t = 2.3/2.4). Their own summary: "through 320M the differences from both baselines are within noise, and
at 60M a parameter-matched standard residual is slightly better"; the effect is "scale-emergent".
They also report that the convex (Highway) form reintroduces vanishing gradients and fails beyond ~20
layers (loss stalls near 6.0 at 40-48 sublayers). Limitations they state themselves: one simple dataset,
undertrained, no downstream eval.

Relation to A: the closest published **precedent for this project's own null result** — a per-channel,
input-dependent residual gate that is worth nothing or less than nothing at small scale under parameter
matching, and only becomes significant at 590M-1B. Directly relevant to (b) and (d).

### 3.5 **arXiv 2609.32534** — "DepthBench: Measuring How Residual Connections Enable More Computational Depth" (Wang, Huang, Kang, González-Martínez, Liu, Liu, cs.CV, 2026-09-26). Upgraded from ABSTRACT (09-29 note) to FULL-TEXT

10 residual designs (Pre-LN, Sandwich-LN, LNS, DeepNorm, KEEL, MoDA, Full/Block AttnRes, HC, mHC) in
OLMo-core on FineWeb-Edu at 20 tokens/parameter, 400M (8B tokens) and 1.6B (32B), **parameter count and
recipe fixed**, with **architecture-specific LR sweeps** over {5e-4, 1e-3, 2e-3, 5e-3} (LNS over
{2e-3 .. 2e-2}) and every architecture reported at its own best.

The directly relevant result for (c): **Table 4 optimal learning rates** — Pre-LN 2e-3, Sandwich-LN
2e-3, LNS 1e-2, DeepNorm 1e-3, KEEL 2e-3, MoDA 2e-3, Full AttnRes 2e-3, Block AttnRes 2e-3,
HC 2e-3, **mHC 2e-3**. On this grid mHC does **not** shift the optimal LR relative to Pre-LN at 400M,
and "the optimal learning rate is consistent across width-depth shapes within each architecture".
Their mHC finding: "mHC performs best at L=24 and does not retain HC's gains in deeper, narrower
models"; composed residual products have effective rank **1.44-1.65 for mHC vs 2.53-2.81 for HC**, and
at L=32 mHC's propagated stream directions become nearly collinear. Mechanism they propose: exact
doubly stochastic maps share the uniform matrix `U = (1/m) 11^T` as a fixed point
(`A U = U A = U`), so repeated mixing drives the product toward `U` — "stabilizing residual transport
can reduce the diversity of long-range routes."

Two caveats I verified: the LR grid is coarse (2.5x steps), and the per-LR losses are only in Figure 12
(an image), so **I could not read how gracefully mHC degrades above its optimum** — which is the exact
quantity this project's LR-tolerance claim is about. Their mHC LR sweep also used a "Legacy
near-uniform init, which differs very slightly from the results reported in the main text using
0/-8 gap-8 init" (their words).

### 3.6 **arXiv 2607.28611** — "Chimera: Designing and Chinchilla-Scaling Hybrid Visual Diffusion Transformers" (Ge et al., 12 authors, cs.CV, 2026-07-30). FULL-TEXT (§3.6, ablation)

Introduces **iHC (Identity Hyper-Connections)**: mHC with the residual-stream transition **fixed to the
identity**, keeping token-dependent read and write vectors `h_pre(R) in (0,1)^M` and
`h_post(R) in (0,2)^M` generated by one `W_h in R^{2M x Md}` projection with a learnable scalar
`alpha_h`, `M = 4`, biases initialised so that suppressing the dynamic term gives
`h_pre = (1/M) 1` and `h_post = 1`. Their own framing: "iHC instead fixes `H_res = I_M` ... This
removes the per-token `M x M` residual mixing matrix and the Sinkhorn projection required by mHC."
Cumulative ablation at 2B: Chimera-dense needs 2.55e20 FLOPs to reach training loss 0.149; + MoE
1.76e20; **+ iHC 1.50e20**; + HeteroP 6.27e19.

Relation to A: no channel or head axis at all (per-branch scalars), so it is not prior art for A — but
it is the **second** independent production-scale design to delete `H_res` outright, and the project's
report should cite it next to the Qwen GR ablation when arguing that the per-group doubly stochastic
mix is the least valuable part of the operator.

### 3.7 **arXiv 2607.24653** — "Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence" (Kimi Team, cs.CL, 2026-07-27, v2 2026-08-07). FULL-TEXT (residual section)

Negative evidence for (a), and worth one line in the report: a 2.8T-parameter MoE published **five days
after MHAR** ships Full/Block Attention Residuals with a **single** learnable pseudo-query
`q_l = w_l in R^d` per layer (`alpha_{i->l}` a softmax over RMSNorm'd sources, shared across all
channels), 8 blocks of 12 layers. So the frontier model did not adopt the head split, although the same
report does use per-head/channel-wise machinery elsewhere (KDA channel-wise forget gate, MLA
channel-wise output gate, Per-Head Muon). This is an absence of adoption, not evidence that the split
does not work.

### 3.8 **arXiv 2603.07461** — "The Dual-Stream Transformer: Channelized Architecture for Interpretable Language Modeling" (J. Clayton Kerce, Alexis Fox, cs.CL, 2026-03-08). FULL-TEXT

A hierarchy Identity ⊂ Independent ⊂ Kronecker ⊂ Dense over attention-head channel groups, applied to
the **V/O/FFN projections** rather than to connection coefficients: "Independent mixing enforces
block-diagonal projections where each head operates in isolation ... The intermediate Kronecker
strategy (`W_heads ⊗ I`) permits scalar mixing between heads while preserving within-head structure
... only `H^2` parameters rather than `(H d_h)^2`." Costs at 29M parameters: fully independent +7.9%
validation loss, Independent-Dense +3.3%, Kronecker-Dense +2.5% versus dense; "FFN mixing contributes
more to performance than attention mixing". Head-count sweep `H in {4,6,8,12,16}` at `D=512`: loss
2.86 -> 2.80 while a specialisation metric rises 0.42 -> 0.85.

Relation to A: the clearest published statement of the **cost** of block-diagonal structure on the
channel axis — the opposite direction from A (A *adds* block-diagonal freedom to coefficients rather
than *restricting* projections), but the same algebra, and the Kronecker row is the natural analogue of
design D vs design A.

### 3.9 **arXiv 2609.33203** — "Structured Residual Connectivity Matters for Diffusion Transformers" (Liu, Ma, Fang, Liu, Wang; NUS/SJTU; cs.CV, 2026-09-27). FULL-TEXT (§3)

The only architectural paper among the 17 new citing papers. AttnRes-style depth routing in DiTs with a
sparse "mirror" pattern (each decoder layer attends to its mirrored encoder layer). Routing uses a
**single learnable vector `w in R^d` per site**: `s_i = w^T RMSNorm(v_i)`, `alpha = softmax(s)` — one
scalar per source, shared across all channels; no head split, no `H_res`, no manifold constraint.
REPA-XL/2 improves 5.9 -> 4.34 FID (1.39 with CFG) at < 0.1% extra parameters and up to 1.73x fewer
iterations. Adjacent depth-routing work; not prior art for A.

## 4. Re-verification of the three claims the report leans on

### 4.1 MHAR (arXiv 2607.27230, now **v2**, 2026-07-31) — head split and U-shape: **confirmed**, with five nuances

Confirmed verbatim: they "partition the coordinates into `H` contiguous heads of width `d/H`, give each
head its own query, and let each head route independently", with
`alpha^{(h)}_i = softmax_i(q_h^T RMSNorm(s_i)_{[h]})` and
`h~_{[h]} = sum_i alpha^{(h)}_i s_{i,[h]}`; parameter-matched (the `H` queries form an `(H, d/H)`
tensor with exactly `d` parameters) and FLOP-matched; `H = 1` recovers the parent. Table 1 reproduces
the project's numbers exactly (anneal corpus, 20K steps, unified peak LR 5e-4): 100M 3.031 / HC 2.999 /
single-head 2.970 / H=8 2.969 / H=16 2.996; 350M 2.997 / 2.931 / 2.876 / **2.848** / 2.895;
1B 2.894 / 2.807 / 2.759 / **2.754** / 2.825. The over-splitting cost grows with scale
(+0.026 / +0.047 / +0.071 for H=16 vs the optimum).

Nuances the project's notes do not yet carry:

1. **The U-shape's depth is corpus-dependent.** On FineWeb (Figure 5a, 1B, KV=8): H=1 3.270, H=4 3.132,
   H=8 3.129, H=16 3.173 — H=16 is +0.044 over the optimum but **still well below single-head**. The
   paper says the penalty "on the anneal corpus of Table 1 ... is larger and H=16 does land above
   single-head routing". The report's "16 heads give back a third to a half of the gain" is right for
   the anneal corpus, not universally.
2. **On web data at a tuned LR, single-head AttnRes is *worse than baseline* at 350M-1B, and HC's gain
   nearly vanishes at 1B.** Appendix Table 10 (FineWeb-Edu, peak LR 1e-3, pre-zero-init-fix):
   100M baseline 3.336 / HC 3.322 (−0.014) / single-head 3.297 (−0.039) / MHAR 3.287 (−0.049);
   350M 3.290 / 3.260 (−0.030) / **3.345 (+0.055)** / 3.210 (−0.080);
   1B 3.175 / 3.172 (**−0.002**) / **3.315 (+0.140)** / 3.111 (−0.063).
3. **The head split survives per-method LR tuning** — this is the nuance that matters most for the
   project's framing. Appendix Table 11 sweeps peak LR per method over {1e-4, 5e-4, 1e-3}: baseline, HC
   and MHAR prefer 1e-3; single-head prefers 5e-4 at 350M and 1B, "whose single shared query is
   destabilized by the higher rate". Best-vs-best, MHAR still wins at every scale
   (−0.049 / −0.080 / −0.063) and single-head at its own kinder 5e-4 still sits **above** baseline
   (+0.038 at 350M, +0.105 at 1B). Their conclusion: "The advantage is a property of the architecture,
   not of a favorable or under-tuned learning rate." They add a 3x-budget check (60K steps) where the
   gaps persist (MHAR −0.056 / −0.062), and a 3-seed paired check under a single shared 5e-4 recipe
   (MHAR −0.045 / −0.090 / −0.071, "exceeding ten standard errors everywhere"; single-head −0.043 ->
   −0.005 -> **+0.093**, and by far the highest-variance method, its three 1B paired deltas spanning
   +0.04 to +0.16 versus MHAR's ±0.002; HC within noise at 1B, −0.016).
   So in MHAR's setting the split both lowers loss at each method's own optimum **and** removes
   variance — the project's "the heads buy LR tolerance, not loss" holds for mHC-over-copies at d384
   but is not the general story for head-split depth routing.
4. **New in v2, Table 7: head alignment buys nothing.** MHAR (arbitrary contiguous subspaces) vs
   MHAR-HW (aligned to KV head groups) at matched head count, FineWeb-Edu, single-seed final-step:
   100M 3.330 vs 3.333 (+0.003), 350M 3.225 vs 3.223 (−0.002), 1B 3.213 vs 3.229 (+0.016); all inside
   their ±0.07 eval-noise floor. They are careful: "we read it as a failure to find a benefit from
   head-alignment, not as evidence of equivalence." Useful for design A: there is no published reason
   to align the `h` groups to the attention heads.
5. **Convergence signature.** In a deliberately under-trained grid (100M, 5K steps, KV=1) H=1 is worst
   and H=8 best, monotone, a 0.028 drop: "Before convergence, more heads only ever help. Trained to
   convergence, the optimum instead saturates into a flat H=4-8 basin." Relevant when reading this
   project's short runs.

### 4.2 SiHC (arXiv 2609.33895) — static per-channel maps and FID 11.50 -> 10.10: **confirmed exactly**

Confirmed at the source: S persistent states each embedding one subpatch and predicting its local
velocity; identity carry `H_mix^l = I_S`; and "Static feature-wise access ... Learned,
input-independent maps `H_pre^l, H_post^l in R^{C x S}`, shared across spatial cells, let each channel
read its own combination of states and distribute its update with its own weights", with
`h^in_{l,c} = sum_s H_pre^l[c,s] h^{(s)}_{l,c}` and
`h^{(s)}_{l+1,c} = h^{(s)}_{l,c} + H_post^l[c,s] delta h_{l,c}`.
Table 4 (B-size, 16x16 patches, direct v-prediction): plain DiT 139.83 -> mHC with copied inputs
(4 states) 25.36 -> mHC with spatial inputs (8x8) 13.25 -> + identity carry, scalar maps **11.50** ->
+ feature-wise maps (SiHC) **10.10** -> + 4x4 subpatches (16 states) 8.07. The project's
**11.50 -> 10.10 claim is exactly right**, and the progression is effectively parameter-matched:
"Parameters and GFLOPs remain nearly unchanged across the progression". One run per row, no seeds.
Also confirmed: "Dynamic maps must be recomputed from the full S-stream state before every update,
which requires materializing the wide state at every sublayer. With static maps and identity carry,
reads and writes instead compose across a stage, allowing exact kernel fusion", lowering peak training
memory from 30.18 to 19.82 GiB. SiHC-XL + REPA reaches FID 1.71 on ImageNet 256^2.

**Correction (bibliographic, not substantive):** the paper's **title** is
"Residual-Stream Burden Shapes Representation Learning in Diffusion Transformers" (Tongtong Liang, Siqi
Kou, Ziqiao Xi, Esha Singh, Kun Zhou, Zhijie Deng, Alexander Cloninger, Yu-Xiang Wang, Rahul Parhi;
cs.CV; "Under review"). "Spatially Indexed Hyper-Connections / SiHC" is the method name inside it.
`REPORT.md` §3 currently reads as if SiHC were the title; `web_and_code_search.md` §7 has it right.

### 4.3 `lucidrains/hyper-connections` `mHCv2(num_fracs = m)`: **confirmed** (repo unchanged since 2026-05-13)

Read the source again. `num_residual_streams_fracs = num_residual_streams * num_fracs` and
`num_input_views_fracs = num_input_views * num_fracs`; the static parameter is
`cat((init_alpha0, eye(num_residual_streams_fracs)), dim=-1)`, so the residual block is an identity
over the **joint** `(stream, fraction)` index of size `n*m`; `residual_mix_constraint_fn` is
`sinkhorn_knopps`, which asserts `log_alpha.shape[-2] == log_alpha.shape[-1]` and alternates
`l1norm(dim=-2)` / `l1norm(dim=-1)` for 20 iterations. The mix is therefore **one
`(n m) x (n m)` doubly stochastic matrix over the joint index — design D — with no option to restrict
it to block-diagonal (design A)**. `H_pre` becomes `(n m) x (v m)`; `beta` (= `H_post`) has shape
`(streams, frac_in, frac_out)`, is `sigma(dynamic + static) * 2` and is **not** manifold-constrained;
with `static_beta = ones(...)` every `(stream, frac_in, frac_out)` entry starts at
`2 sigma(1) ~ 1.46`, i.e. every output fraction is written into every fraction of every stream, and the
read mixes all `n*m` pieces at init. The report's sentence about the `m > 1` initialisation is correct.

### 4.4 Also re-verified: ExoFormer is now **v4** (2026-05-27), and its granularity result is more mixed than the report implies

`REPORT.md` says ExoFormer "ablates scalar vs head-wise vs element-wise mixing coefficients on
attention projections; per-channel coefficients self-organize into head blocks." Both halves are
correct in v4 — "three coefficient granularities for lambda: Scalar (S) ..., Headwise (H) (one
coefficient per head, broadcast across `d_k`), and Elementwise (E) (one coefficient per channel)", and
"For the value pathway (V), the heatmaps exhibit sharp boundaries that align precisely with head blocks
... despite the optimizer having the freedom to set each channel independently", with Q/K/G showing
finer intra-head "striping". But the measured ordering is **not** monotone in granularity:

| configuration (~450M, FineWeb-Edu, Muon, 5-shot) | avg acc | PPL |
|---|---|---|
| E-NuResFormer / H- / S- (full residual norms) | 49.68 / 49.42 / 49.11 | **14.15 / 14.17 / 14.17** |
| E- / H- / S-NuResFormer (only Q/K norms) | 48.91 / 49.68 / **49.83** | 14.21 / 14.21 / 14.22 |
| E-ExoFormer / H- / S- | **49.85** / 49.23 / 49.16 | **14.13 / 14.14 / 14.15** |
| Dynamic E-ExoFormer | 50.27 | 14.09 |

Their reading: "Among NuResFormer configurations, scalar mixing achieves the highest average downstream
accuracy (49.83%) despite having the fewest parameters ... Elementwise mixing yields the best language
modeling perplexity (14.15 ...) but slightly lower downstream accuracy than its scalar counterpart.
This suggests increased parametric freedom can improve in-distribution loss but may not generalize as
well." And for ExoFormer: "elementwise mixing (E-ExoFormer) achieves the overall best performance ...
This reversal indicates that the effectiveness of coefficient granularity is architecture-dependent."
Note that the 49.83% scalar win comes from a *different normalisation configuration* ("only Q/K
norms"), so within matched settings perplexity does improve monotonically with granularity while
downstream accuracy does not. Also relevant to (c): their gradient-spike score is 0.0054 for
E-ExoFormer vs 0.0085 for gated attention and 0.0108 for the naive combination.

## 5. Corrections and refinements to earlier claims

1. **"16 heads give back a third to a half of the gain"** (REPORT §3, from MHAR): true on MHAR's anneal
   corpus; on FineWeb at 1B, H=16 is +0.044 over the H=4-8 optimum but still far below single-head.
   State the corpus.
2. **"MHAR's results are the best prior for A"** — still true, but the report should carry MHAR's
   Appendix Tables 10-12: on web data single-head AttnRes *regresses* versus baseline at 350M (+0.055)
   and 1B (+0.140), HC's gain at 1B is −0.002, and **MHAR's advantage survives per-method LR tuning,
   a 3x budget and three paired seeds**. The project's exp10 conclusion ("the LR robustness is the
   heads', not the parameters'", heads' gain gone at 0.5x LR) should therefore be stated as a result
   about *this* architecture and scale, explicitly contrasted with MHAR's tuned-LR result, not as a
   general claim about head splits.
3. **MHAR v2 adds a head-alignment null** (Table 7): aligning heads to KV groups gives no measurable
   benefit, single-seed and within noise. Nothing in the literature supports aligning design A's groups
   to attention heads.
4. **SiHC title** (§4.2): the arXiv title is "Residual-Stream Burden Shapes Representation Learning in
   Diffusion Transformers"; SiHC is the method. Fix the REPORT §3 bullet and the BibTeX.
5. **DepthBench was labelled SUMMARY/abstract-only in §7** and summarised as "HC and Full AttnRes keep
   improving at deep-narrow shapes". Now read in full: it also reports (i) **mHC's optimal LR = Pre-LN's
   2e-3** on a {5e-4, 1e-3, 2e-3, 5e-3} grid at 400M, (ii) mHC "performs best at L=24 and does not
   retain HC's gains in deeper, narrower models", (iii) effective rank of the composed residual map
   1.44-1.65 (mHC) vs 2.53-2.81 (HC) with a Birkhoff fixed-point explanation. (i) is evidence *against*
   a general "mHC shifts the usable LR upward" claim, although the grid is coarse and their mHC LR
   sweep used a near-uniform init.
6. **`web_and_code_search.md` §3.2's characterisation of `wdlctc/hyper-connection-factory` as a
   laptop-scale smoke test is now out of date** (§2.2): it has 303M and 1.21B runs as of 09-29/10-02.
   Its caveats (single seed at L/XL, one shared LR per size, uniform mHC init, depth/width confound)
   should be stated whenever its numbers are used.
7. **ExoFormer is v4, not v3** (2026-05-27), and the granularity ordering is architecture-dependent and
   non-monotone in downstream accuracy (§4.4). The report's one-line summary is defensible but should
   not be read as "finer is better".
8. **`H_res` is now an actively discarded component in three independent designs** (Qwen GR, Chimera
   iHC, SiHC identity carry), with Qwen stating "`H_res` adds little. Once the read and the write are
   expressive enough, adding the `n_r x n_r` mixing operator brings no significant improvement."
   The report's design-A framing (whose novelty is precisely a *per-group* `H_res`) should engage with
   this directly.
9. **Metadata fixes for the variant zoo**: sHC/s2HC (2603.20896) is now **v2, 2026-09-25, accepted to
   NeurIPS 2026**; Hyperloop Transformers (2604.21254) is v3 (2026-07-02); JPmHC's title is
   "JPmHC Dynamical Isometry via Orthogonal Hyper-Connections" (2602.18308); MV-Split's title is
   "Mean Mode Screaming: Mean--Variance Split Residuals for 1000-Layer Diffusion Transformers" and its
   author is **Pengqi** Lu (lucidrains' commit message spells it "Penqi").
10. **The 09-26 crawl's real weakness was screening, not coverage.** Four of the items in §3
    (2608.30320, 2606.05014, 2606.31859, 2607.28611) are present in `notes/literature/raw/s2_cites_*.json`

    title/abstract keywords.
11. **Possible production adoption beyond DeepSeek**: a third-party kernel compiler implements
    `Glm53HyperConnection` alongside `DeepseekV4HyperConnection` (§1.3). Unverified against any GLM
    publication; do not state it as fact without a primary source.

## 7. Bottom line

**Design A still unpublished as of 2026-10-03: yes.** Nothing in the 2026-09-28 .. 2026-10-03 window
changes that, and the wider sweep found no paper or repository that gives each channel group its own
read, write and doubly stochastic residual mix.

**Closest new item:** **Qwen3.8-Next's Gated Residual (arXiv 2608.30320, 2026-08-31)** — design A's
read half at `h = D` on a 4-branch widened stream, with a per-branch-scalar write, no `H_res`, plus the
three ablations the project most needs (read granularity helps, write granularity does not, `H_res`
adds little) and a zero-spike result at 4x the optimal learning rate. Runners-up: Chimera's iHC
(2607.28611) as a second `H_res`-free production design, Depth-Attention (2606.05014) for a quantified
per-head-vs-uniform decomposition, and `wdlctc/hyper-connection-factory` (2026-10-02) for an
independent parameter-matched MHAR-vs-single-head result at 303M.

**For the report's own claims:** the per-channel read granularity that design A generalises is now
*published and shipped* at the `h = D` limit (Qwen GR, SiHC), and it helps there; the per-group
`H_res` that makes design A novel is the component three independent groups have chosen to delete; and
the "heads mainly buy LR tolerance" framing must be scoped to this project's setting, because MHAR's
head split demonstrably survives per-method LR tuning.
